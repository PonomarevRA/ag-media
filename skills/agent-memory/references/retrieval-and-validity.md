## Dates and conflicts

The object's `recorded`/`updated` dates describe the memory record. They do not prove when a product version became active or when an observation was made. Record an explicitly known validity interval or observation date in the existing payload text and cite its source. When unknown, say so; do not recover dates by guessing from prose or file modification time.

When two current sources disagree, preserve their attribution and conditions and mark the disagreement for review. A later memory edit is not automatically the winner. For a historical question, distinguish the time the assertion describes from the time the evidence became known. A current file path or source revision establishes traceability, not factual truth or permission.

Use existing `related` and `supersedes` links and their established meanings. Replace an accepted decision only with evidence of a changed decision; do not silently rewrite history or add incompatible top-level fields to represent a temporal model. Separate independently changing claims when that makes validity and sources clear.

## Procedures in a new environment

A procedure card points to its reviewed runbook, preconditions, verification and tested environment. For a different runtime, tool or model, treat it as a candidate requiring review. Check the target's capabilities, permissions and preconditions; a matching family label does not establish compatibility. Tie evidence to the exact procedure/runbook revision. A metadata test is not an execution, and a reported successful application in one environment does not establish transfer success elsewhere.

Store a real application result as an attributable outcome in the existing format. Preserve unknown/partial results and failures; do not invent independent tasks, success or approval to improve metrics.
